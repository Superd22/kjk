Likeness HQ

This page shows how well our head system can reproduce a real face, zone by zone. For each zone it says what is done, what we still do not like, and what we want to try next.

The goal. Show that the sliders of our character designer can reproduce any face.

So each zone gets two verdicts. The first asks whether the sliders can make that part of a face. It is judged against 15 heads whose true 3D shape we know, with every slider set as well as it can be. The second asks how well a single photo sets those sliders. The first verdict is the one the goal is about.

Face shape

Sliders GoodPhoto Partly

The overall shape of the head comes from 300 sliders taken from FLAME, a model learned from about 3,800 scanned heads. They set how wide and long the face is, the cheeks, the forehead, and where the nose and mouth sit.

Done

  • Set from a true 3D head, the sliders come within 1.7 mm of the face and 1.4 mm around the jaw, on average.
  • From a front photo, our head's outline lies 3.9 mm from the true head's, against 6.1 mm for the average head.
  • A profile photo, when there is one, brings the profile from the nose to the chin to 0.87 mm.
  • The sheet now draws our head turned left or right as the photo's head is, so the two can be compared in place.

What we don't like

  • A head tilted forward looks much like a short face in one photo. The fit tilts most heads about 4.5° chin down, which is most likely not real.
  • The skull above the eyes is the same dome on everyone.
  • How deep the eyes sit is the average on everyone. A front photo holds no depth.

Next ideas

  • Test the base layer against the true heads with each point free to slide along the surface, as the nose tests already do.
  • Find a better way to tell a tilted head from a short face, perhaps from the profile photo.
  • Read the eye depth from a three-quarter photo or from shading.

Jaw and chin

Sliders GoodPhoto Weak

The jaw and chin are set by the same 300 sliders. A separate jawline part with five controls was built and then switched off, because it made the photo fits worse.

Done

  • Set from the true 3D heads, the sliders make these jaws to about 1.4 mm, with a defined edge.
  • The jawline part adds nothing on the true heads. Its controls stay within 1.5 mm of zero.

What we don't like

  • The face detector draws a round line at every chin. On the photo of #901 it is round where the chin is flat.
  • Pointed chins are not found. #005 is the test case.
  • Chin width wants +6.4 mm on average, so the base head's chin is too narrow.
  • A beard confuses the outline, as on #004.

Next ideas

  • Build the average chin width into the base head.
  • Read the chin's edge from the photo's own shading, the way the nose's width is now read.

Nose

Sliders PartlyPhoto Partly

The nose has the page's 25 nose sliders, two nose controls learned from the 15 true 3D heads, and a fixed correction built into the base head. Most of today's work was here.

Done

  • The round lumps that every head had at the nostril corners are gone. A fixed correction learned from the 15 true heads removed them.
  • Set from the true 3D heads, the sliders make the nose's width within about 1 mm, from the wings up toward the bridge.
  • From a photo, the width at the wings is now read from the picture itself. Our head lands within 0.7 mm of the photo, and its widths follow the photos with a correlation of 0.99.
  • Faint creases under the wings came from a fault in the test, not from the sliders, and are fixed.

What we don't like

  • Our tips are broader and rounder than the real ones. Lincoln's tip is flat, and #901's and #902's tips are pointier, with a bulge just above #901's.
  • Our noses have no groove between the tip and the wings, so the lower nose reads as one broad block.
  • Apart from the width, no nose slider is read from a photo yet.

Next ideas

  • Measure the tip and the groove on the 15 true heads, check whether the sliders can make them, and add a slider only where they cannot.
  • Then read the nose sliders from the front and profile photos.

Eyes

Sliders PartlyPhoto Partly

Each eye has controls for the width, height and place of its opening, and 14 numbers for the curve of its lids. The iris has a size, a colour and a pupil size. Newer sliders shape the lid: the crease's height and depth, a fold of skin over the inner corner, how full the upper lid is between its edge and the brow, and how deep the eye sits.

Done

  • Over about 105 photos, our lid outline lies 0.30 mm from the photo's, and the width between the eye corners matches.
  • The iris colour and the pupil size are set by drawing our head and measuring its eyes the same way as the photo's. #005's iris used to be 3 times too bright against the white of the eye; now it matches within 0.002. Pupils match within 0.03 of the iris's width.
  • The pink inner corner no longer breaks into a light wedge where the lids leave the eyeball, as it did on #003.
  • An inner fold, a crease, upper lid fullness and eye depth can now be set. Fullness and depth are smooth 3D sliders, with no creases from 0.5 back to fully forward.

What we don't like

  • The new lid sliders are not read from photos, so on the sheet #005 still has a crease-less but hollow lid and a visible pink corner.
  • Our lids have no visible thickness, so the white of the eye ends in a hard edge, and dark eyes look lighter than on the photos.
  • In profile the upper lid's edge has a small pointed hook.
  • Learning lid sliders from the true 3D heads failed: in that area the scans record eyebrow hair and lashes as skin.

Next ideas

  • Read the crease and the inner fold from the front photo, and the lid's fullness and depth from the profile photo.
  • Paint the lids' edges and their shadow on the white of the eye.

Mouth and lips

Sliders PartlyPhoto Partly

The mouth is placed by the face's shape sliders. The lips are paint, set by 14 sliders. Six sliders set the upper lip's outline: its height, the bow's depth, width and sharpness, its sides and its corners. Four set the lower lip's outline: its height, the width of its bottom, its sides and its corners. Four set the lip colour against the skin: lightness, redness, warmth and the upper lip's shade.

Done

  • The bow of the upper lip now differs between people. It used to look the same on everyone, because the old paint followed the face detector's outline, which is close to an average mouth.
  • On our heads the upper lip's outline is 0.52 mm from the photo's on average, against 0.89 mm with the old paint. The lower lip's is 0.33 mm, against 1.07 mm.
  • The lower lip's colour against the skin matches the photo exactly, and the upper lip's is within about 3 of the smallest steps a person can see. With the old fixed colour they were 14 and 22 steps off. #903's red lips, #002's pink lips and #006's mauve lips come through.
  • With a profile photo, how far each lip stands forward is right within about 0.5 mm.

What we don't like

  • On photos where the head is tilted, our mouth sits too low, so the gap between nose and lip looks too long, as on #006, #010 and #012. This is parked.
  • The lips are flat and matte. The photos show shine and texture.
  • #006's lower lip is fuller in 3D than her upper lip. A front outline cannot show how far a lip pushes forward or how round it is.
  • A lopsided bow, as on #901, can only get a symmetrical one.

Next ideas

  • Sliders for how far each lip pushes forward and how full and rolled it is, tested against the 15 true 3D heads.
  • Lip shine.
  • Test whether the sliders can place the mouth right on the true 3D heads, with no photo involved.

Eyebrows

Sliders GoodPhoto Good

An eyebrow is hair drawn on the brow ridge. Seven numbers set its line and thickness, plus its two ends, its colour and two grooming settings. Each side has its own numbers, so uneven eyebrows stay uneven.

Done

  • The seven numbers leave only 0.19 mm of an eyebrow's height unexplained.
  • Our eyebrows' thickness and darkness follow the photos with a correlation of about 0.8 to 0.9.
  • An eyebrow hidden by hair, as under #003's fringe, is refused and copies the other side or the average eyebrow.

What we don't like

  • A raised eyebrow and a high eyebrow cannot be told apart.
  • The hair is drawn for the front view, so it is not quite right on a turned head.

Next ideas

  • Store the hair on the skin so it turns with the head.
  • Set the ridge from real depth.

Lashes

Sliders PartlyPhoto Partly

The lashes are paint drawn from the lid edge. Four numbers set how dark the line on each lid is and how far the fringe reaches. They were left at this state on purpose.

Done

  • Our upper lash line follows the photos with a correlation of 0.85, and how far it reaches with 0.72.

What we don't like

  • The lash colour is one dark brown on everyone.
  • The lower lid measure counts the shadow under the eye as lashes.
  • Eyeliner cannot be told from a strong natural lash line.

Next ideas

  • A lash colour.

Ears

Sliders WeakPhoto Not yet

The ears have had no work of their own. In an earlier test on twelve 3D scans, the sliders of that time closed only 27% of the gap between the average ear and the scanned ears. That test was before the 300 FLAME sliders were added, and it has not been run again.

Done

  • Nothing yet.

What we don't like

  • The ears are close to the same on everyone.

Next ideas

  • Run the slider test on the ears against the 15 true heads with today's sliders.

Skin and hair

Sliders Not yetPhoto Not yet

On the test sheet our skin now takes the colour of the photo's cheek, below the eyes. It had to come with the lip colour, because a lip colour is a difference from the skin: on one peach skin for everyone, #002's pink lips came out red-orange. These are three colour numbers set from the photo, not sliders yet, and the whole face gets one colour. Hair is not part of this work yet. The chosen direction for hair is a bald base head with hair caps that must fit all 14 headgear items.

All numbers come from the test scripts in ~/.claude/tools/likeness-fit. The full reference, with every control, its range and its score, is docs/research/2026-11-17-head-parts-and-controls.md in the kjk repository.

The true 3D heads are the three Star Wars heads, Lincoln's life mask, the Lee Perry-Smith head and ten people from Meta's Multiface set. None of the Star Wars or Multiface data may ship.

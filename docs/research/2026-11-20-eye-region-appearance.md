# Eye region appearance for procedural paint

This note collects measured facts about how a real eye region looks, so that the skin paint around the eye of a kjk head reads as realistic at conversation distance.

Each claim has a link. A claim marked "(read)" was taken from the page text of the paper. A claim marked "(search summary)" was only seen in a search result summary and should be checked in the paper before we rely on it. Where no primary measurement exists, the note says so.

## What this means for our eye paint

1. The upper lash line should be a dark band about 0.07 mm thick per lash, but at conversation distance it merges into a continuous band. Draw it as a band roughly 1 to 1.5 mm thick at the middle of the lid, based on the lid margin being about 2 mm thick and the lashes being 5 to 6 rows. This band width is our own inference and no study measured it.
2. Draw the lower lash line thinner and lighter than the upper one. The lower lid has 75 to 80 lashes against 90 to 160 on the upper lid, and the lower lashes are shorter.
3. Put the upper crease 6 to 8 mm above the lash line for most Caucasian and Black faces, with women about 0.5 to 1.3 mm higher than men. For East Asian faces use 4 to 6 mm, or no crease at all.
4. Under-eye darkness should be a band centred on the lower lid and inner half of the lower eyelid, following the orbital rim. A realistic average is 4 to 14 L* units darker than the cheek.
5. Use two colour recipes for under-eye darkness. The constitutional type is brown to black and mostly lowers L*. The vascular type is blue, pink or purple and mostly raises a* by about 5 units while barely changing L*.
6. Make the darkness stronger at the inner and middle thirds of the lower lid than at the outer corner. The study that measured zones used the middle of the lower eye contour as its main zone.
7. Add a skin lightness change with age and with cosmetics. Faces look younger with more contrast between the eyes and the skin around them.
8. Do not darken the whole upper socket as a fixed pigment. Real upper eyelid darkness is a mix of shadow, which depends on light, and pigment, which does not. Paint a modest pigment band and leave the shadow to the lighting.

## 1 Lash line geometry seen from the front

### Lash length

The mean upper lash length of women falls with age, from about 8.0 mm at 22 to 29 years to about 6.4 mm at 50 to 65 years (search summary of a study of 179 women, [Glaser et al. 2014 on SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/glaser2014/68417061)).

In a bimatoprost trial an increase of 1.62 mm was reported as 24 percent of upper lash length, which gives a baseline near 6.8 mm (calculated from [PMC4003339](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4003339), read).

A general summary gives typical upper lash lengths of 3 to 10 mm, and says lashes of 12 mm or more are called trichomegaly (search summary, [Wikipedia, Eyelash](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Eyelash)).

The idea that upper lashes are 8 to 12 mm long is therefore too high for an average person. A length of 7 to 8 mm is typical, and 8 to 10 mm is a long but normal lash.

Lashes grow about 0.12 mm per day, with an anagen phase of about 34 days (search summary, [Thibaut et al. 2010](https://academic.oup.com/bjd/article/162/2/304/6642272)).

### Lash diameter

The mean lash diameter is about 71.7 micrometres in Asian women and about 61.0 micrometres in Caucasian women (search summary, [Na et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17107385/)).

An X-ray study of Japanese women found elliptical cross sections with a mean major axis of 77.8 micrometres and a minor axis of 63.2 micrometres (search summary, same result list as above).

One lash is thus about 0.06 to 0.08 mm wide. A single lash is far thinner than one texel on a head texture, so the lash line has to be painted as a soft band and not as drawn lashes.

### Lash count and rows

The upper lid has 90 to 160 lashes and the lower lid has 75 to 80, according to [StatPearls on the eyelash](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537278/) (search summary).

Another source gives 90 to 120 upper lashes in five to six rows and 75 to 80 lower lashes in three to four rows ([Elsevier anatomy on eyelashes](https://www.elsevier.com/resources/anatomy/eye-accessory-visual-structures/eyelids/eyelashes/15653), search summary).

The [NCBI eyelid anatomy chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482304/) gives roughly 100 upper and 50 lower lashes, so the lower count is disputed (search summary).

Asian women have fewer lashes but thicker ones, and their lashes have lower curl and lift angles (search summary, [Na et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17107385/)). No paper in this search gave numeric angles.

### Lid margin and waterline

The eyelid margin is about 2 mm thick. Its back edge is covered by conjunctiva and holds the openings of the meibomian glands, and its front edge is covered by skin ([Pathology Outlines](https://pathologyoutlines.com/topic/eyeeyelidanatomy.html), search summary).

The gray line lies between the skin and the conjunctiva. The gland openings sit behind it and just in front of the mucocutaneous junction ([Entokey, anatomy of the eyelids](https://entokey.com/anatomy-of-the-eyelids/), search summary).

No primary study that measures the width of the visible pink waterline was found. The 2 mm figure covers the whole margin, so the visible pink part is likely under 1 mm at normal view distance. That is our inference.

### Caruncle and eye opening

The caruncle is a soft pink oval body about 5 mm high and 3 mm wide, lying in the tear lake medial to the plica semilunaris ([Taylor and Francis ophthalmology chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/9780429166860/chapters/10.1201/b19589-18), search summary).

The adult eye opening is about 30 mm wide and 10 mm tall (same source). Another teaching source gives 28 to 30 mm wide and 9 to 11 mm tall ([anatomy of eyelids slides](https://de.slideshare.net/drdipankardebcardio/anatomy-of-eyelids-78977938), search summary).

A 3 by 5 mm pink patch on a 30 mm wide eye is one tenth of the eye width. It is clearly visible in a close view and still shows at conversation distance as the pink inner corner.

### Lash thickness in photographs

A study of upper lash photographs from above reports thickness as an area in square millimetres, with a baseline near 0.8 mm squared. The bimatoprost change of 0.35 mm squared was reported as 44.8 percent, which gives a baseline of about 0.78 mm squared ([PMC4003339](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4003339), read). Thickness by age ranged from 1.17 mm squared in the 50 to 65 group (search summary, Glaser 2014 above). The two sets of numbers use different methods and are not directly comparable.

No study that measures the apparent width of the dark lash band seen from the front, or how it changes from the inner to the outer corner, was found. The note cannot give a measured inner to outer profile. A reasonable artistic choice is a band that is thinnest at the inner corner and thickest at the outer two thirds, because lashes are shorter and sparser at the inner corner, but this is not backed by a source here.

## 2 Upper lid crease

Crease height is measured from the lid margin to the crease. Methods differ between studies, so values are only roughly comparable.

[PMC5665901](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5665901) (read, Tables 2 and 4) gives these values.

| Group | Men (mm) | Women (mm) | Source in that table |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caucasian | 6.9 | 7.3 | Cartwright et al. |
| Caucasian | 6.2 | 7.5 | Price et al. |
| African American | 7.2 | 7.7 | Price et al. |
| Asian, unspecified | 6.6 | 6.5 | Park et al. |
| Chinese, Singapore | not given | 6.0 | Sim, Smith and Chan |
| Chinese, Taiwan, 21 to 30 years | not given | 6.0 | Chen, Ma and Liao |
| Chinese, southern Thailand | not given | 4.0 | Preechawai |
| Malay, southern Thailand | not given | 6.6 | Preechawai |
| Malay, 3D measurement | 8.05 | 8.56 | Table 4 of the same paper |
| Chinese, 3D measurement | 3.95 | 5.89 | Table 4 of the same paper |

In every Caucasian and African American row except one, women have a higher crease than men. The difference is 0.4 to 1.3 mm. Our suggested "7 to 10 mm in men" does not appear in these data. Men are at 6.2 to 7.2 mm.

In Chinese women the crease is 4 to 6 mm and in Chinese men it is about 4 mm. Many Chinese subjects have no visible crease, and the Malay and Chinese study counted how many had a double eyelid ([Malay and Chinese eyelid study](https://eprints.um.edu.my/18989/1/The_Prevalence_of_Double_Eyelid_and_the_3D_Measurement_of_Orbital_Soft_Tissue_in_Malays_and_Chinese.pdf), search summary only, prevalence not read).

Patients asking for Asian blepharoplasty chose crease heights of 6 to 8 mm with a median of 7 mm. This is a preference and not a population norm (search summary, [PMC6571304](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571304)).

### How the crease and the tarsal platform look in tone

No paper in this search measured the lightness of the crease, the tarsal platform (the strip of upper lid skin between the lash line and the crease) or the lid fold. The following is anatomy only.

Eyelid skin has no subcutaneous fat and is the thinnest skin on the body ([StatPearls eyelid anatomy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482304/), search summary).

The skin of the upper lid is thinnest near the lash margin at about 320 micrometres and about 860 micrometres at 7 mm above the lashes (search summary, [Asian Journal of Surgery abstract list](https://dbpia.nl.go.kr/asj/article/35/8/1007/249004)). Thin skin lets the blood in the muscle underneath show through, which is why the tarsal platform often looks a little pinker or more purple than the skin near the brow.

The crease is a fold, so it receives less light than the platform below it when light comes from above. That makes a thin shadow line at the crease in most photographs. This is a general optical argument and not a measurement.

## 3 Periorbital hyperpigmentation (dark circles)

### Colour

The Annals of Dermatology study ([PMC8460477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8460477), read) describes constitutional circles as a band of brown to black pigmentation. It describes vascular circles as bluish veins under thin skin.

The review at [PMC4756872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4756872) (read) lists these colours.
- Brown or dark brown for pigmented macules.
- Blue, pink or purple for vascular types.
- Grey or blue-grey for dermal melanocytosis.
- Skin coloured for structural shadows made by the shape of the face.

The same review says vascular darkening is stronger on the inner part of the lower lid. It says stretching the lower lid separates pigment, which stays, from shadow, which fades.

### Where it lies

The Singapore classification found that most patients had darkening on both the upper and the lower lids. Constitutional darkening follows the shape of the orbital rim (search summary, [Sunway Medical listing of the classification paper](https://www.sunwaymedical.com/en/research-publications/classification-and-characteristics-of-periorbital-hyperpigmentation); [Asians epidemiology study](https://read.qxmd.com/read/21682796/periorbital-hyperpigmentation-in-asians-an-epidemiologic-study-and-a-proposed-classification)). The 65 percent figure for both lids comes from this summary.

The tear trough shadow is centred over the lower inner rim of the orbit ([PMC4756872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4756872), read).

No study found gave a distance in millimetres below the lash line. The colour study at [PMC6927230](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6927230) (read) used three zones along the lower eye contour. Zone 1 starts at the inner corner, zone 2 is in the middle of the lower eye contour, and zone 3 is further out. A cheek zone served as control.

### How its darkness is measured

[PMC8460477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8460477) (read) is a study of 100 people, with a mean age of 41.5 years and 69 women. It used a Konica Minolta CR-400 chromameter at the lower eyelid and the mid cheek, both on the mid-pupil line.

The study defined the darkness as the lower lid L* minus the cheek L*. Mean values were these.

| Subtype | L* difference | a* difference | b* difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constitutional | 3.75 | not reported | not reported |
| Post-inflammatory | 11.7 | 4.57 | -1.04 |
| Vascular | not significant | 4.89 | 1.59 |
| Shadow | not significant | 3.30 | not reported |

The vascular and shadow types did not change L* significantly. They changed a*, which means more red. The L* difference correlated with how unhappy people were about their dark circles at r = 0.35.

[PMC6927230](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6927230) (read) studied 12 Caucasian women with dark circles and 12 without. Mean ages were about 35 and 33 years. Values from VISIA images in zone 2 and on the cheek were as follows.

| Group and zone | L* | a* | b* |
|---|---|---|---|
| With dark circles, zone 2 | 50.1 | 16.7 | 21.3 |
| With dark circles, cheek | 64.1 | 16.9 | 24.2 |
| Without dark circles, zone 2 | 58.4 | 12.4 | 18.3 |
| Without dark circles, cheek | 67.1 | 15.7 | 20.8 |

Three facts follow from this table.
- In women with dark circles the under-eye is 14.0 L* units darker than the cheek.
- In women without dark circles the under-eye is still 8.7 L* units darker than the cheek.
- The under-eye is less yellow (lower b*) than the cheek in both groups. It is less red (lower a*) in the group without dark circles, and equally red in the group with dark circles.

The same paper found a melanin index of 0.61 under the eye against 0.36 on the cheek in the dark circle group. The haemoglobin index was 0.72 against 0.34. So both melanin and blood are higher. The skin in zone 2 was 0.9 mm thick in the dark circle group against 1.1 mm on the cheek (ultrasound, read).

The photonumeric scale paper ([PMC7898348](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7898348), search summary) says the grades of its 10-point scale correlated with CIELAB and ITA values at r above 0.9. I could not read the page, so no numeric grade table is given here.

### Age and skin tone

Tear trough depression is age related, caused by fat loss, skin thinning and the cheek moving down ([PMC4756872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4756872), read).

In the 100 person study, the most common type was vascular at 38 percent, then constitutional at 34 percent, shadow at 12.2 percent and post-inflammatory at 3 percent ([PMC8460477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8460477), read). The paper gives the numbers inconsistently in its text.

A study of 1,204 Indian women found most had darker periorbital areas (91 percent), including on both lids ([Hourblin et al. 2014, Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/489648290/hourblin2014), search summary only). A pilot study in South Asians found that clinical severity went with spectrophotometric melanin ([Pigment International 2021](https://doaj.org/article/2fef4371fa8e427f91d9f89c29cd4972), search summary).

No paper in this search gave L* differences split by Fitzpatrick type. The review text says dark circles are common in people with dark pigmentation or Mediterranean ancestry ([PMC3483573](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3483573), search summary).

## 4 The upper socket under the brow

No paper found measured the L* of the upper socket (the hollow between the lid fold and the brow bone) against the cheek.

What the sources say is limited to these facts.
- Shadow effects, from an overhanging fold, eye bags or a deep tear trough, make up about 11 to 12 percent of dark circle cases in the Singapore study (search summary, links in section 3).
- Dark circles on the upper lid are very common in the Indian sample, and pigment on the upper lid and orbital rim follows the orbit shape (search summary, section 3).
- Light from above leaves the socket in the shadow of the brow. This is a standard optical argument and no measurement was found.

The practical reading is that the socket is darker than the cheek in most photographs, mostly from shadow, and that a smaller part comes from pigment in people with constitutional darkening. The stretch test described above (pigment stays, shadow fades) is the only primary distinction we found.

## 5 Lightness profile across the lid

No study found measured a continuous L* profile from the lash line across the upper or lower lid in photographs.

Related measurements that do exist are these.
- Russell measured contrast of the eyes, brows and lips against their surroundings in CIELAB. Women have more contrast around the eyes and lips than men, because women's skin is lighter, while the eye and lip luminance do not differ much by sex ([Russell 2009 summary, Perception](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3590275), search summary only).
- His group's method labels the eyes including a narrow band of skin around the lashes, and the annuli around the eyes (search summary of the same group of papers, [Swansea repository](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa26118/Download/0026118-11022016150518.pdf)).
- Contrast decreases with age in Caucasian women, and faces with higher contrast look younger (search summary, Porcheron et al. 2013, cited in the same sources).
- Cosmetics raise facial contrast ([Bangor repository entry](https://research.bangor.ac.uk/portal/en/researchoutputs/cosmetics-alter-biologicallybased-factors-of-beauty-evidence-from-facial-contrast(26ef7bd7-724f-4d78-ada8-16df637ba727)/export.html), search summary).
- The lower lid is on average 8.7 L* darker than the cheek even without dark circles, and 14.0 darker with them (table in section 3, read).

For our paint this means the lower lid zone should always sit below the cheek in lightness. We have no measured shape for the upper lid, so we need to fit that profile ourselves from KOTOR reference heads.

## Gaps and what to do about them

These items have no primary source in this search.
- The visible thickness of the dark lash band from the front and its change from the inner to the outer corner.
- The width of the visible waterline.
- The tone of the crease and the tarsal platform.
- The L* of the upper socket against the cheek.
- A measured lightness profile across the lid.
- Dark circle L* differences split by skin tone.

The lower lid and cheek numbers in section 3 are from small samples of mostly Caucasian women. Treat them as the strength of the paint at its maximum, and scale by skin tone from the KOTOR head references.

- Say which roadmap item your work serves (A1 to A5, a zone's B1 to B5, a C step, a D step, or tooling).
- Test on the base head and the 57 KOTOR test heads only, never on random slider settings.
- Write every text for a person by the rules of `CLAUDE.md` at the root of the kjk repo: plain full sentences, one fact per sentence, with real numbers.
- The tools are in `~/.claude/tools/likeness-fit/` and their data in `~/.claude/tools/likeness-fit-data/`. Run Python as `.venv/bin/python -I` from the tools folder. The trial scripts and their README are in `scan/trials-2026-11-18/`.
- Do not change the default behaviour of a script that others use. Add an option that is off by default, or write a new script.
- Write anything that will ship in the mod in Rust (decision 22). Write anything that is heavy to compute in Rust too, whether it ships or not (decision 53). Python is only for light work, such as reading files, preparing inputs and gluing steps together. Older Python tools stay until they are ported.
- A step is not done until the user can see its result in the head viewer, on our head next to the KOTOR original, for every one of the 57 heads. Numbers and picture sheets go alongside the viewer, never instead of it. The user asked for this on 2026-11-19.
- Look at pictures of a result before believing its numbers. Every fault found so far was in the targets or the method, not the sliders.
- Do not commit, and do not change the kjk repo, unless the user asks.
- Work that runs builds, tests, clippy, benchmarks, slider solves, renders or the game is done by a full conversation, started by the conversation that leads the area. It is never handed to a sub-agent, because a sub-agent loses its cache after 5 minutes and most of these commands take longer. A conversation launches sub-agents only for work that never waits on a long command: research, judging pictures, code review, quick lookups or a browser check. The user set this on 2026-11-20.
- A conversation started to do a task does it without asking the user for a go-ahead. It stops for the user only when something goes against its brief, or when a choice would change what is being built. The user set this on 2026-11-21.
- When your work changes what is done or what comes next, update "Now and next" below.

## The goal

Players get a head customization system that is varied enough to be interesting, and that fits KOTOR's look and feel.

We think the FLAME head model can be the base of that system, with controls of our own added to it. The roadmap below is how we prove that FLAME works. The human heads of KOTOR 1 and 2 are the test set: they show the variety that KOTOR's look allows, and the style we must keep.

## A. Global

These must hold for the whole head, whatever the player picks.

1. **Our head moves like KOTOR's heads in the game.** That means body animations, lip sync, head turns, glances and blinking. The rig contract (`2026-11-16-head-body-rig-contract.md`) lists what a head must keep. This is considered validated. The new computed blink has only been checked in the Python tests, not in kjk.
2. **Nothing breaks.** Helmets, masks, goggles and hair caps must still fit, and collisions must not need redoing. Our head must stay inside the original head's volume. This will probably be one of the last steps, because it sets the ends of the slider ranges.
3. **Our head loads in kjk as a KOTOR model**, with the nodes the rig needs, wearing a texture.
4. **Our head fits a vertex and performance budget** that the low-end GPU target can run (`2026-11-18-minimum-gpu-target.md`).
5. **Our head looks like KOTOR in the engine.** This covers its shading and texture resolution, and how smooth its surface looks. In the test renderer, our head looked lower in detail than KOTOR's even though it has more vertices. Most of that was our paint, which was one colour per vertex. Since 2026-11-19 our head has its own texture, which removed most of the blur. KOTOR's textures have lighting painted into them, such as the dark under the nose. Whether our paint copies that painted-in shading is decided once we are in the engine.

At each step, check whether something is missing from this list.

## B. Zone by zone

The zones are the forehead, the brows, around the eyes, the nose, the cheeks, the mouth, the chin and the jaw. The ears and the skull may follow. Zones will probably be split further once the work reaches them. Each zone goes through five steps.

1. **Variety.** Does KOTOR have enough variety in this zone? If every KOTOR head has nearly the same mouth, reproducing the mouths proves little. So a zone's variety is measured by how far apart the KOTOR heads are from each other in that zone.
2. **Reproduce.** Can our head reproduce that variety? A fit that lands close to each KOTOR head is not enough on its own. Our fitted heads must differ from each other as much as the KOTOR heads do, and in the same ways.
3. **Fix.** Fix what we cannot reproduce, with new controls or learned shapes.
4. **Paint.** This step moved to its own track, part C, on 2026-11-19.
5. **Make it shippable.** The zone must still work with fewer vertices, and through sliders a player can use rather than FLAME's raw weights. When this step happens, and whether it is done per zone or for the whole head, is still open.

At each step, check whether something is missing from this list.

## C. Paint

Painting is its own track since 2026-11-19, and it runs in parallel with part B. Paint lives in our head's texture layout, which does not change when the sliders move vertices, so it does not wait for the shapes.

**The test.** Carrying KOTOR's texture onto our head is only a check of the shape. The real test is whether our own procedural paint can recreate KOTOR's faces. The quality to aim for is the hand-made textures of the mods, not only the game's own textures. The targets are the KOTOR textures baked into our own layout by `kotor_bake.py`, one per test head. Our procedural texture is in the same layout, so the two compare pixel for pixel. For each part of the face, we fit the paint's settings to every KOTOR head, then measure the colour difference, the sharpness and whether each feature is there. Variety is measured as in B1 and B2: how much KOTOR's paint varies in a region, and how much of that variety our fitted paint keeps.

**The layers.** The paint is a stack of layers, each with a mask whose edge fades out:
1. skin tone, with broad regional tints such as redder cheeks, nose and lips, darker eye sockets and a beard shadow;
2. fine skin detail, such as pores, and wrinkles that grow with age;
3. the lips: their colour, and how sharp or soft their border is;
4. the brows;
5. eye makeup, liner and shadow (the lashes go on the lash strip);
6. facial hair, from stubble to beards;
7. details placed like decals, such as freckles, moles, scars and tattoos.
Paint may be asymmetric, so the layout must not mirror one half of the face onto the other.

**Built from the head, not from pixels.** Every layer is computed from the head's 3D surface: its landmarks, the vertices of the lip border, distances to the eyes, and noise sampled in 3D. It is then baked into the texture. When a slider reshapes the lips, the lip colour follows them. The texture layout can also change without redoing any paint work.

**The layout.** Our head's current layout spends little of its texture on the face. It fills 24% of the texture, and gives the lips 4.8 pixels per square millimetre at 2,048 pixels. Bastila's mod texture gives her lips 42 at the same size. So our head needs a new layout that gives the face most of the texture. Its seams must sit where they do not show, such as behind the ears, under the chin and under the hair.

**Comparing.** At every step the viewer can show our head with KOTOR's texture carried over, with our procedural paint, or with no paint. Each layer can be switched on and off.

**The order.**
1. A new texture layout, and sharper targets baked into it.
2. Skin tone and regional tints, fitted to the 57 heads. These are broad, so they can start on the current layout.
3. The lips, with the first seam, between the lips and the skin.
4. Then brows, eye makeup, facial hair, fine detail and decals.
5. Later: the body's skin coloured to match the head (user's decision, 2026-11-20). With a KOTOR body under the head in viewer 2, our paint is darker than the body's skin at the collar, and Carth's body texture is only 256 pixels square.

## D. Hair

Hair is its own part since 2026-11-19. The research is in `2026-11-19-drawn-and-procedural-hair.md`, and painted hair is covered in `2026-11-18-painted-facial-hair.md`.

**Hair groups (decision 61).** Scalp hair will use the same hair groups as facial hair: curves on a grid with their own sliders, shaped by dragging control points, never by brush painting. The facial hair work in part C builds them first, on the face grid. Part D takes them over to the scalp and adds volume (the cap, the cards and later long beards).

**The system.** A hairstyle is made from a few drawn curves and sliders, not picked from a library of meshes. The player's curves sit on a hair shell, which is the scalp pushed outward by a height that sets the volume. Some curves show the flow, one marks the parting, and others make ponytails. A solver spreads their directions through a coarse grid around the head (Fu et al. 2007), and strands grow from the scalp along that flow. Curl and wave come from a twist along each strand.

**Three layers, one flow.** The same flow drives every layer, so they agree on direction and parting.
1. Painted hair, in the head's texture: the hairline blend on every style, and buzz cuts, temples, sideburns and the hair near the ears. It is a paint layer in `head_paint`.
2. A hair cap: a thin shell over the scalp with a strand texture, for short and medium hair.
3. Cards: strips for loose hair, such as bangs, locks, ponytails, buns and long hair.

**The test.** We do not need KOTOR's hairstyles to vary much, because new styles are easy to invent. Reproducing a few of KOTOR's hairstyles is still the test that the system can make KOTOR's look. The first milestone is Bastila's and Carth's hair made by our system from a few curves, shown in viewer 2 next to KOTOR's.

**The order.**
1. A prototype with no drawing. Curves and sliders come from small files, the Rust code makes the mesh and the paint, and viewer 2 shows the result.
2. Reproduce three to five KOTOR hairstyles by hand, then perhaps take the flow from KOTOR's own hair meshes.
3. Drawing in a viewer or in kjk, once the choice of decision 31 is made.

## Tooling

Everything else serves A and B without being a goal itself. Most of the work done up to 2026-11-19 is tooling. The tools are in `~/.claude/tools/likeness-fit/scan/trials-2026-11-18/`, and their README lists each one.

- **Targets.** 57 KOTOR heads (45 game heads, Kreia, Bastila and 12 mod heads) are wrapped with our mesh (`kotor_hairless.py`).
- **The fit.** `kotor_fit.py` fits FLAME, with our eye opening controls, to each head. `kotor_fit_check.py` proves that the fit finds heads that the sliders make themselves.
- **Closeness.** `kotor_fit.py` scores each zone as the mean distance from our fit to its KOTOR head, with the 95th percentile, over the KOTOR skin only.
- **Variety.** `kotor_variety.py` measures B1 and B2 for each zone. It gives how far apart the KOTOR heads are, how far apart our fits are, and whether the same heads differ in both.
- **Identity.** `kotor_judge.py` checks that each fitted head is nearer its own KOTOR head than any other.
- **Headgear fit.** `kotor_judge.py` measures how far our head lies outside the KOTOR head and its hair. Its first results are not trusted yet (see below).
- **Distance maps.** `kotor_judge.py` colours each fitted head by its distance from the KOTOR head.
- **Pictures.** `kotor_paint.py` and `kotor_blink_sheet.py` make textured sheets in the test renderer. Those only check the method. Likeness is judged in the kjk engine.
- **Zone galleries.** `kotor_gallery.py` shows all 57 heads cropped to one zone: KOTOR's heads shaded and textured, our fits, and distance maps.
- **Mouth animation.** `kotor_talk.py` plays KOTOR's lip sync shapes and expressions on our fits with KOTOR's face bones.
- **The head viewer.** A 3D viewer shows any KOTOR head next to our fit of it, with one camera for both. It can show KOTOR's paint, plain shape or (later) our procedural paint, and it animates our head with KOTOR's clips and a voiced line. Its data comes from `kotor_viewer_export.py` and `kotor_bake.py`.
- **Texture bake.** `kotor_bake.py` bakes each head's KOTOR paint into a texture in our head's own layout. These textures are the targets of part C.
- **Likeness HQ.** The status site is served from this Mac at http://127.0.0.1:8765/, with one page per zone. Its sources and scripts are in `~/.claude/tools/likeness-fit/hq/`.

## Decisions

Every decision taken about this work, newest last.

1. **3D heads only.** Since 2026-11-18, sliders are tested on 3D meshes only, not photos. The goal is to reproduce every human head in KOTOR 1 and 2.
2. **KOTOR heads, never random settings.** Tests use the base head and the KOTOR fits. The values the 57 fits use will set each slider's range, and the sliders stop at those ends. What breaks beyond them is fixed or cut then.
3. **The pass mark.** A head passes when its face is within 0.5 mm of the KOTOR head, no zone is worse than 0.75 mm, and it is nearer its own KOTOR head than any other. The mark may be tightened if heads still do not look right. Variety (B1 and B2) is judged next to it.
4. **Eye openings.** FLAME gets 26 opening controls per eye, outside its own shapes. The fit holds the target's size fixed, so KOTOR's eye slit fits round our eyeball.
5. **The blink.** The closed lids are computed on each head from its own lid edges. It is validated in the Python tests first. Whether the engine computes it or the sliders carry it is decided later.
6. **The lash strip.** The lashes and liner go on a thin strip along the lid edge, with its own sliders outside FLAME.
7. **Eyes paused.** The eye work (lid paint, lash strip, control ranges) is paused so the rest of the face comes first.
8. **The roadmap.** The work is split into part A (global), part B (zone by zone) and tooling. Every result is framed by the item it serves. At each step we ask whether A or B is missing an item.
9. **Headgear comes late.** Our head must stay inside the original's volume so helmets, masks and hair caps fit (A2). This is checked late, because it sets the ends of the slider ranges. The planned "skull hold" was dropped. Hair-covered skin is already left out of the closeness score.
10. **Zones.** The eight zones stay as they are for now. They will be split when the work reaches them.
11. **Likeness HQ.** The status site has a page per zone. It is served from this Mac, not from claude.ai, because the head viewer's data is too large to publish and a page on claude.ai cannot read local files.
12. **The head viewer.** The viewer is the main tool for judging likeness: pick a head, compare KOTOR's original with ours, and animate ours. It keeps the ability to show KOTOR's original texture on our head.
13. **Zone pages give zone numbers.** A zone's page shows the zone's own shape variety (the zone lined up on itself), not whole-face numbers.
14. **Our head has its own texture.** Paint is baked into a texture in our head's layout, not stored as one colour per vertex, which was blurry.
15. **Paint is its own track (part C).** It runs in parallel with part B. Its test is whether our procedural paint can recreate KOTOR's faces.
16. **The paint quality to aim for is the mod textures**, not only the game's own textures.
17. **Paint may be asymmetric.** The texture layout must not mirror one half of the face onto the other.
18. **Paint is built from the head's 3D surface** and then baked, so it follows the sliders and survives a layout change.
19. **A new texture layout.** Our head needs a layout that gives the face most of the texture, to reach mod-texture detail at 2,048 pixels.
20. **Painted-in shading waits for the engine.** Whether our paint copies KOTOR's painted-in lighting is decided once we are in the engine.
21. **One plan.** This note is the plan for us and for every agent. The work in progress and the next steps are kept in its "Now and next" section, each with what it serves and what "done" means.
22. **Rust for what ships.** Everything that will ship in the mod is written in Rust from 2026-11-19 on, starting with the paint system. That covers the paint generator and its layers, the computed blink, applying the sliders to the mesh, baking a texture and skinning with KOTOR's bones. The analysis tools that never ship (the wrap, the fit, the measures, the galleries) may stay in Python for now. When they need what ships, they call the Rust code instead of keeping a Python copy of it. Porting tools to Rust for speed is lower priority, and starts with profiling where the time goes.
23. **Where the Rust code lives.** For now the paint code is the crate `head_paint`, in `crates/character_customisation/head/head_paint`. The folder `crates/character_customisation` is itself a small crate, because the workspace's `crates/*` pattern needs every folder it matches to be a crate. It is the entry point for character customisation and re-exports `head_paint`. The workspace lists `crates/character_customisation/*/*` as members. This layout is not final and will move before the mod ships. The work is on the branch `head-paint`, in the worktree `.claude/worktrees/head-paint`, and nothing is committed unless the user asks.
24. **Paint analysis in Rust too.** On 2026-11-19 the user asked for the skin tone analysis to be written in Rust as well, because Python is slow. New analysis tools for the paint track are written in Rust, next to the code that ships, so both share the same colour maths and mesh code. The existing Python tools stay until they are ported.
25. **Mouth shape after paint.** On 2026-11-19 the user deferred the mouth's shape work (B3) until the paint is done, because the paint may help, and the shape is refined only if needed. The user also judged that KOTOR's mouths vary enough (B1).
26. **Skin tone from pigments, with room for colours skin cannot have.** The base tone and broad tints start from two to four pigment-style controls, such as melanin and haemoglobin, the way Baldur's Gate 3's skin uses haemoglobin, melanin, vein and yellowing maps. Grey-green, blue and other colours real skin never has are allowed, for aliens and stylised faces.
27. **Painted shading depth waits for the engine.** KOTOR's biggest tint pattern is the depth of its painted shading. A control for it, computed from the head's shape, is probably needed, but the user wants to see the faces in the engine first (A5).
28. **Our own paint only needs our layout.** The per-pixel bake that reads KOTOR's texture is only for making targets. Our procedural paint is computed at each pixel's point on our head and written into the same layout. Both share the step that finds each pixel's point on our head.
29. **Colour regions, tentative.** The regional tints use about 10 soft regions, following MetaHuman's list (scalp, forehead, nose, under the eyes, cheeks, lips, chin and ears) with the eye socket split into upper and lower. Each region has a left and a right amount, linked by default and unlinked for asymmetry.
30. **One unnatural-colour control for now.** A single control moves the whole face toward green, blue or other colours real skin never has. Regional tints get a hue of their own. More controls come only if the fits need them.
31. **The paint viewer first, then a choice.** The viewer first shows one baked texture of our paint per head, with switches for each layer. When the user wants to edit the paint live, we decide whether the prototype stays in the browser or moves into kjk.
32. **Masks are computed from the head.** Every paint layer's mask is computed from the head's shape, such as distances to landmarks, with sizes and positions that can be sliders and can be fitted. No mask is painted by hand. The user agreed on 2026-11-19 and will revisit this if hand-painted masks are ever needed.
33. **Hair is part D.** Hairstyles are generated from drawn curves and sliders through a flow field, then baked to painted hair, a hair cap and cards. They are not picked from a library of meshes. KOTOR's hairstyles need not vary much. Reproducing a few of them is the test of the look.
34. **Headgear turns the hair mesh off.** When a character wears headgear, the hair cap and the cards are hidden, and the painted hair stays. The user is not worried about fitting hair inside helmets.
35. **Painted hair is a paint layer.** The hairline blend, buzz cuts, temples, sideburns and the hair near the ears are painted into the head's texture, along the same flow as the mesh hair.
36. **The first hair prototype has no drawing.** Curves and sliders come from files, and viewer 2 shows the result. Drawing waits for decision 31.
37. **The hair's strand texture belongs to part D,** made by our own code.
38. **Patents are checked before shipping,** not before prototyping. Hair meshes and Choe and Ko's wisp model are patented, and Fu's method has not been searched.
39. **Players start from a preset head, then edit.** The creator offers 4 random KOTOR player heads for males and 4 for females as starting heads, and the player tweaks from there. Blending presets together is still ruled out (decision of 2026-11-16).
40. **The first named sliders follow recognisability.** They are chosen from the shape features that most change how a face is recognised (Abudarham and Yovel 2016, in `2026-11-16-likeness-recreation.md`), such as lip thickness, eye shape and size, ear protrusion, forehead height, jaw width, nose size and shape, face proportions, cheeks and chin. Hair colour, eye colour, brows and skin marks are paint.
41. **Editing prototypes run in the browser, with the logic in Rust.** This settles decision 31. Every rule that edits the head, its paint or its hair lives in the Rust crates, compiled to WebAssembly for viewer 2 and natively for kjk. The viewer's JavaScript only draws and handles input, so what is built in the prototype ports to the engine. The look is still judged in the engine (A5).
42. **The first editing milestone.** In viewer 2, the player picks one of the 8 starting heads, then moves 13 named shape sliders (lip thickness, eye shape, ear protrusion, forehead height, eye size, jaw width, nose size, nose shape, face proportions, cheek shape, chin shape, eye spacing and mouth size) or drags about 5 handles (nose tip, chin, jaw corners, mouth corners). One Rust solver, compiled to WebAssembly, turns every edit into FLAME weights plus our own controls, holding unedited measurements and keeping the face plausible. Paint, hair, the computed blink and the face bones follow the edited head live, because nothing that works today may break, animation included. An offline report says how close the 13 sliders plus dragging get to each of the 57 KOTOR heads.
43. **Smoothness before exact likeness.** The bar is three things. The starting heads look smooth. Editing with sliders keeps them smooth. Sliders can mostly recreate every KOTOR face. Fitting KOTOR's heads exactly is not the goal, because the KOTOR heads are proof that the system is good enough, not faces to replace. A small loss of closeness for smooth skin is acceptable, as long as the identity test still passes and the heads still read as their KOTOR originals.
44. **Our head ends where KOTOR's player heads end.** Our mesh still carries a neck and shoulders. It is cut to the neck line of the heads a player can pick in KOTOR, so it joins the bodies the game expects. Every one of our heads is cut at that same place, with the same vertices, whether it was fitted to a player head, an NPC head or a mod head. The cut's edge is held fixed by the smoothing step and by every slider, so the join to the body never moves.
45. **The eye area is generated from curves.** Pushing a generic lid mesh's vertices with stacked fixes made the skin fold, split and bulge. So the area within about 15 mm of each eye is built by code from a few curves: the lid edges, the corners, the lid fold and the hollow under the brow. The curves are fitted to the Eyes v3 targets, and their parameters become the eye-shape sliders. FLAME still places the eyeballs and shapes everything outside that area. The lids are separate basic strips along the curves, as KOTOR does, so the blink rotates them and no lining shows. The user asked for this on 2026-11-19, with the curve-generated area as the priority.
46. **No more eye shape rounds for now.** Six eye rounds on 2026-11-19 fitted the shape of each eye to KOTOR and none was accepted. An audit found that KOTOR's eye is mostly paint, that no liner, lash line or lash strip exists in our code, and that the lid edges had been fitted to the outer edge of KOTOR's paint, which makes our bare eyes stare. The user agrees that the default smoothed head has the most natural eyes. The user also finds them not perfect and too small, even counting the lash strip to come. The default opening shows 79% of KOTOR's visible area. Eyes are judged mostly at conversation distance. They must also hold up in the editor, but they need not be perfect at 5 cm. The eyes come back after the nose, as sliders tested like every other feature, with KOTOR's visible eye as the target and not the edge of its paint. Two ideas are untested: a rule in the head's build that lays the lid skin against the eyeball, and a plain placeholder lash strip so that eye size can be judged.
47. **A head is nothing but its slider values.** The same slider value gives the same result on every head. A starting head is a saved list of slider values, with no shape of its own underneath. This follows the rule of 2026-11-18 that everything must be doable through sliders. A few sliders are global, such as face length, and those move every feature.
48. **Four tests of the sliders.** First, power: agents prove that the sliders can make each KOTOR feature, with a full optimiser. The user does not copy faces by hand. Second, variety: the slider-made heads keep KOTOR's variety, and faces sampled inside the ranges the KOTOR heads use are believable and differ from each other. The ranges may be widened by a margin if more variety is wanted. Sampling inside those ranges is allowed for this test, which decision 2 did not allow before. Third, feel: the user judges whether editing is fun. Before that, agents check that a slider does not move other zones, that moving a slider up and back returns the same head, and that no slider is dead or a twin of another. Fourth, nothing breaks: the blink, the mouth, the paint, the hair, the neck rim and the smoothness hold on edited heads.
49. **Measure features, not millimetres.** An average distance between two surfaces does not prove that a nose reads as the same nose. Power is scored per feature and per head, as "can the sliders make this nose", from proportions (where the feature sits and how big it is, as ratios of the face) and shapes (outlines from the front, the side and below, compared after position and size are removed). A measure is proven before it is used, by four checks. It ignores what nobody notices. It sees what the user notices, and the step the user first notices becomes the pass mark. It tells KOTOR's heads apart correctly. The pairs it calls most alike and most different look right to the user.
50. **Sliders may be learned from the KOTOR heads and named by hand.** The user's goal is that building a head is fun and that the system is powerful, and reproducing KOTOR's heads is the proxy for powerful. Several ways of making sliders are compared by the tests of decision 48. The first bet is directions learned zone by zone from the KOTOR heads, with soft edges between zones.
51. **The nose first, then the eyes.** The nose does not move, reads from its shape and not its paint, and has depth. The sliders, the measure and the optimiser are Rust, in the crate `head_sliders`, and the same code runs in the browser from the start, because the smoothing step exists only in Rust. This applies decision 41.
52. **Shape polish waits for the spine, and paint carries on.** The user agreed on 2026-11-19 to pause likeness work on the shape of single features until one head goes from sliders to kjk. That covers further eye and mouth shape rounds and the hair. Paint is not paused. The user leads it in a second session, in parallel, because paint lives in the texture layout and does not wait for the shapes (part C). The nose correction learned from scans stays for now: the user considers the licence fine for a mod, and the nose has not been reworked for KOTOR yet. Nothing is committed yet, because where the code will live is not settled.
53. **No Python for heavy computing.** On 2026-11-19 the user asked every agent to avoid Python for everything that is heavy to compute. That covers fits, optimisers, measures run over many heads, bakes, solves and anything that loops over vertices or pixels, whether it ships or not. Such work is written in Rust. Python stays for light work: reading KOTOR's files, preparing inputs once, and gluing steps together. This widens decisions 22 and 24.
54. **Folds and wrinkles are two things.** A fold is a crease that anatomy places and that exists at any age: the nose-to-mouth fold, the mouth corner fold, the chin crease and the under-eye crease. A wrinkle is a line that comes with age, such as forehead lines and crow's feet. Folds are built first, because most of the 57 heads have folds and only a few have wrinkles. A fold's path is computed from the head it is painted on, so it moves when the nose wing or the mouth corner moves. Fold sliders do not depend on age. The user agreed on 2026-11-19.
55. **Creases come from a generator, and the fitter guesses its sliders.** The plan to detect each crease in each KOTOR texture and fit every curve per head is dropped. A generator makes the creases from a few sliders. The fitter measures a KOTOR face per feature (is the crease there, how dark, how long, how sharp, how many) and finds the slider values that give the same measures. It never fits points or lines one by one. The bar is a crease that feels like the KOTOR head's, not one on the same pixels. Three rules hold. A small change of the head's shape gives a small change of the creases and never reshuffles them. The same values on the same head always give the same pixels. The same values on another head give the same pattern adapted to that head. Widths stay in millimetres. The user asked for this on 2026-11-19.
56. **Wrinkle patterns come from style sliders, not a bare seed.** The first candidates are spacing (few lines far apart or many close together), continuity (long unbroken lines or short broken ones) and waviness, plus one small variation value for what is left. A wrinkled KOTOR head such as Kreia becomes a saved preset of these values. Age is stored, and each region's slider means more or fewer wrinkles than usual for that age, so dragging age keeps the player's tuning. Two points wait until the user has seen a first version: whether there is one pattern control or one per region, and whether neighbouring pattern values morph into each other or jump.
57. **Paint runs live in the browser from the same Rust code.** This applies decision 41 to paint. `head_paint` is compiled to WebAssembly as a module of its own, next to the module of `head_sliders`, and the head's positions pass between the two through JavaScript. The native and the browser builds must paint the same pixels, and a check compares their textures. Every yes-or-no choice in the paint code, such as whether a line forks, comes from a whole-number hash, because the last digit of some maths functions differs between the two builds. The page paints a small texture while a slider moves and the full size on release.
58. **Paint moves to the GPU.** The user decided this on 2026-11-20. Each pixel of the paint is computed alone from its own point on the head, which is the job a GPU is built for, and the processor version needs 8 workers and 864 MB to repaint the full size in half a second. The painter becomes Rust code that drives wgpu with WGSL shaders, in a new crate `head_paint_gpu`. The engine and the browser run that same crate and those same shaders, the browser through WebGPU. Rust on the processor keeps what is not per pixel: the landmarks, the per-vertex fields, growing the lines and the lookup tables. The shaders do everything per pixel. The aim is a full repaint at 2,048 pixels in under 50 ms, so the full texture follows a slider while it moves. This changes decision 57 in one point: byte for byte equality is no longer required. The same slider values must give the same picture, rounding differences are accepted, and yes-or-no choices still come from whole-number hashes, which are exact on every GPU. Today's processor painter stays for now as the reference the shaders are checked against.
59. **The upper lid is a generated rigid piece.** The user decided this on 2026-11-20. The skin round the eye keeps the default smoothed head's shape, and the upper lid is one piece per eye, made by code from the head after the sliders, like the hair. KOTOR's lid bone turns it for the blink, and the lower lid stays skin. Each piece is made for one eye and mirrored for the other, so the two eyes can later be set apart. A thin lash strip runs along the piece's edge.
60. **Facial hair is drawn on a grid, and sliders are presets on that grid.** The user decided this on 2026-11-20. Facial hair lives on a grid of points on the lower face. Each point holds how dense the hair is, how long it is and which way it grows. The sliders (for example the height of the cheek line or the width of a goatee) write a preset onto the grid, and the player will later be able to draw any shape on it, such as a moustache that hides the upper lip. The brows and facial hair share one primitive, which draws single hairs from such a grid. The flow is the same idea as the hair's coarse grid in part D. Paint stops at short beards for now. Long beards come later, when the work on hair resumes, by taking the hair's grid down to the beard and making a 3D mesh from it. Stubble replaces the beard shadow tint of the skin layer. Every head can have facial hair, and women's heads start at zero.
61. **Hair is made of hair groups, for facial hair and scalp hair alike.** The user decided this on 2026-11-20. A hair group is a curve on the face-anchored grid, with a width that changes along it and its own sliders (length, density, thickness, curl, colour and greying). The hairs grow along the curve. The same hair group makes painted hair (stubble, short beards, buzz cuts, the hairline) or volume (a hair cap, cards and later long beards). A preset, such as a moustache style, is a saved hair group: its curve in grid coordinates plus its slider values. The player adds a preset and tweaks it, or adds a hair group of their own. Hair groups sit in a list in a set order, and each grid point takes its hair from the top group that covers it, so groups never blend into each other. A hair group drawn on one side is mirrored to the other side by default, with a switch to turn that off. The player shapes a curve by placing and dragging control points on the grid, as in VRoid's select tool. Free-hand painting with a brush is ruled out, because the user expects it to feel bad. "Strand" keeps its meaning of one single hair.
62. **Animation calibration is a separate, optional step after the fit.** The user decided this on 2026-11-20. The skin weight rules and the bone regressor are learned from our fitted heads, with KOTOR's motion as the target, and then compute any slider-made head's rig from its own vertex positions. One command runs all of this calibration. It covers the mouth and the brows for now, and any new part that needs calibrating is added to the same command. It is not run on every fit. A stale calibration still runs on a refitted head and only gets slightly worse, but it must be rerun when the mesh changes. The calibration records which fit it was learned from, so the viewer or a test can warn when it is older than the fit. Viewer 2 always shows the current calibration, with no choice between rig versions, so its Mouth choice goes away. The fitting code moves out of the session scratch folders into `head_rig`, in Rust.
63. **Two kinds of hair grid, and the paint grid covers the whole head.** The user decided this on 2026-11-20. The paint grid lies on the skin and matches the face. It covers every place hair can grow: the scalp, the hairline, the temples, the sideburns, behind the ears, the nape, the beard area and the neck. Each point is bound to the texture layout, so it is the same part of the head on every head. The only hard limits are the lips, the eyelids, the nose, the ears and the inside of the mouth. Zones (scalp, hairline, temple, sideburn, cheek, moustache, soul patch, chin, jaw, neck) are labels that set default stubble and default directions, not walls; hair groups own the hair. A volume grid is a 3D grid that conforms to one part of the head, the scalp for hair and the jaw for long beards, and holds the cap, the cards and later long beards. Volume hair is rooted on paint grid points. The hairline is a hair group like any other, drawn by the player or taken from a preset. A hair group is either a stroke (a curve with a width) or a filled area (a closed outline), both shaped with control points; a buzz cut is a filled area over the scalp. When a volume is added, a default rule paints a small outskirt round the volume's footprint, with its hairs pointing toward the volume, as if pulled taut. The paint grid comes first. The AI makes one small test, and the user drives the rest by hand.
64. **The eye is not rebuilt around a larger eyeball set further back.** The lead and David chose the rebuild on 2026-11-21, after rounds 5 to 7. Its first step showed that KOTOR's eyeballs sit about where ours do on pmhc04 and Bastila, and that the change shortens the eyeball's wedge in profile. The eye work goes on with the existing eyeball. The next target is the opening's height and the heavy upper lid.
65. **The eye is not rebuilt around corners on the equator.** The lead chose this construction on 2026-11-21. Its step 0 showed that most of our outer corners already sit there and that KOTOR's inner corners do not. The next eye work targets where the lids cross the iris, above all the lower lid, through new measures in the solve. Bastila's heavy lid needs a thicker lid piece that juts forward, which has to be built without the tab that the prototype made.

66. **Hair direction is set by flow lines that belong to a hair group.** The user confirmed this on 2026-11-21, after round 15 of the facial hair. A flow line is a curve drawn inside a hair group, edited with the same handles as a stroke. Every hair of that group takes its direction from the group's own flow lines, blended by distance from the nearest ones. A group without flow lines keeps the default direction of the grid's zones, and pulls stay as a global adjustment on top. Scalp hair in part D will use the same flow lines.

67. **The eye opening is built from a curve on the eyeball, set by ten handles.** David and Eyes HQ chose this on 2026-11-21, after the preview held up on all 57 heads. The curve replaces the press, the corner carve and the 23 sliders that shaped the opening (the lid points, the corner sliders, Eye width, Eye opening height, Eye tilt, Socket angle, Upper lid cover and Caruncle width). The whole-eye sliders and Upper lid fullness stay. The lid piece turns about a line through the eyeball's centre parallel to the line through the tips. The caruncle comes back later as a cosmetic. It went live in viewer 2 on 2026-11-21, behind the switch `EYE_CURVE_LIVE`, which also undoes it.

68. **The neck's rim never moves, for compatibility with KOTOR's bodies.** David restated decision 44 on 2026-11-21, when a throat prototype could not reach Bastila's throat angle because her rim sits at the bottom of her throat. No slider or construction may move the rim, even when a head's throat cannot be reached.

69. **The back of the neck is out of scope, and the ears come later.** David decided this on 2026-11-21. The back of the neck is fixed by the seam to the body, and no player will want to change it. The ears stay out of every measure until their own step.

70. **A diagnosis measures against a reference, with no judges.** David decided this on 2026-11-21: "we don't need subjectivity yet, we need measurements." A diagnosis step gets or builds a reference for what it measures, then measures against it. Fable judges are kept for prototypes, and David judges in viewer 2.

71. **Haired KOTOR heads get a bald reference.** David asked for this on 2026-11-21. KOTOR models no scalp under modelled hair, so the 21 haired heads have a reference made of KOTOR's real skin and a skull filled in from the 36 heads whose skull shows, kept at least 6 mm under the hair. The fill lands 3 to 6 mm from a real skull when tested by leaving one head out. The skull sliders are solved toward these references.

72. **Clearly better results go straight into viewer 2's default.** David decided this on 2026-11-22: "let's fold our effort directly into the viewer when they're clearly better rather than add an url encoded option, we have too much dead code." A result is clearly better when no head gets worse on any measure, nothing folds at the solved values, and the close-ups are clean. The fold deletes the prototype it replaces and writes how to undo it. Shipping into kjk itself still waits for the going-live steps.

73. **Brow and forehead stay one feature, and the forehead runs into the skull as one measured profile.** The HQ and David agreed on 2026-11-22. One outline from the brow ridge over the forehead and the top of the head is part of the skull solve and guard, so a step at the hairline between the brow feature and the skull sliders shows as a miss.

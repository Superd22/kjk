## The goal

Our upper lid piece is a thin shell. KOTOR's upper lid is a thick ridge that stands out in front of the eyeball, and that is what gives heads like Bastila their heavy, hooded lid. On Bastila, KOTOR's lid juts 8.6 mm in front of her eyeball and ours juts 1.9 mm.

Build the lid piece's thickness as a real, measured, 3D feature, with a slider, solved on all 57 KOTOR test heads. A trial in an earlier step made the lid thicker and it did look heavier, but it left a flap of skin (a "tab") at Bastila's outer corner. The flap must not come back. Decision 65 in the roadmap states this task: "Bastila's heavy lid needs a thicker lid piece that juts forward, which has to be built without the tab that the prototype made."

This step is the first of the two builds that the equator-and-lid note asked for. The second one, an outer corner that reaches the eyeball's equator, is not part of this brief.

## What is known

- The piece is generated by code from the head after the sliders, one per eye, and KOTOR's lid bone turns it for the blink. That is decision 59. The lower lid stays skin.
- The code is the crate `head_eyes`, at `crates/character_customisation/head/head_eyes/src/`. `piece.rs` generates the piece, `piece_check.rs` checks it, `web.rs` is its browser build, `caruncle.rs` is the caruncle, and `src/bin/lid_piece.rs` is its tool. The eye opening's curve lives in the slider kit's `eyes.rs` and `eye_curves.rs`.
- The piece's thickness today is two numbers in `piece.rs`. `LidSettings::min_thickness` is 0.8 mm, the least thickness of the piece's edge. `CURVE_END_THICKNESS` is 0.12, which is the share of that thickness the piece keeps at each of its two ends, and `CURVE_THIN_SHARE` is the share of its length over which it thins at each end. The report prints `thickness_min` and `thickness_max`.
- The earlier trial set `min_thickness` to 2.2 mm instead of 0.8 mm. Bastila's lid read as heavier, and a flap of skin appeared at her outer corner, both open and closed. The flap came from round 7's slider Outer corner reach together with either a heavy Upper lid cover or the thick piece. With Outer corner depth at -4 mm the flap went away. Round 7's sliders were never published, so check what of this is in the live build before you rely on it.
- A thickness of 0.8 mm at the edge is not the same thing as 8.6 mm of jut. Jut is how far the piece's front surface stands in front of the eyeball's centre plane, which a thicker edge changes only in part. Decide early whether thickness alone can reach KOTOR's jut, or whether the piece's front surface has to be lifted off the eyeball as well.
- Two faults in the eye region are already known and are not this step's job, but do not make them worse. There is a black dash under the closed lower lid on Bastila and Carth, which is in the live build too. Our inner corner shows a large pink caruncle where KOTOR shows a pointed white corner.
- The judges of the earlier step said that our eyes are too round and too open next to KOTOR's on every head. That is mostly the lower lid, which is a different step.

## The steps

1. **Step 0, prove the premise on KOTOR's 57 meshes.** Do not build anything yet. Build a measure of how far the upper lid stands in front of the eyeball, read on KOTOR's own head with KOTOR's own eyeball and lid pieces, and on ours. Read it on all 57 test heads and on the base head. The guide's four checks must pass: the left-against-right wobble must be far smaller than KOTOR's spread between heads, the measure must see what David sees on Bastila and on a flat-lidded head, KOTOR's spread must be large, and the heads at each end of the measure must look right in a picture sheet. Beware the trap the earlier step hit: KOTOR's eyeball meshes have only 16 to 33 triangles, so their faces lie well inside the sphere their vertices sit on. Report the medians, the spread from the 10th to the 90th percentile, and our gap to KOTOR, before you go on.
2. **Build the thickness as a slider.** Name it for what a player sees, not for the code. Give it a range that covers KOTOR's spread from step 0. The piece must still close over the eyeball at the blink, still thin out at its two ends, keep its lash strip, and fold no triangle at any value a solve lands on.
3. **Kill the tab.** The flap of skin at Bastila's outer corner is the gate on this step. Find what makes it, in today's curve-based code and not in the old press, and fix it inside the piece's own construction if you can. Show Bastila's outer corner in close-up, open and closed, at the solved value and at the slider's top end.
4. **Solve on all 57 heads.** Fit the slider to every head against the step 0 measure. Report the median gap before and after, how many heads got worse on any measure, and how many fold a triangle at their solved values.
5. **Look at it.** Make the comparison pictures David reacts to: our head next to KOTOR's, open and closed, from the front, the side and three-quarters, with the hair off, on Bastila, pmhc04, Carth, Kreia and at least five more heads that the measure moves most. Use close-ups for the corners. Sub-agents may be used as judges on these pictures, and for nothing else.
6. **Fold it or stop.** Decision 72 is the rule. A result is clearly better when no head gets worse on any measure, nothing folds at the solved values, and the close-ups are clean. If it is clearly better, make it viewer 2's default, delete the prototype it replaces, and write down how to undo it. If it is not clearly better, stop and report, the way the earlier step did. Two to four loops at most. Do not chase perfection.

## How to work

- Get your own worktree and your own port. Make a worktree under `.claude/worktrees/` on a branch off `character-customisation`, then run `sliders/serve_feature.sh eyes <worktree>`. It prints every command with the paths filled in, and takes the next free port from 8770 upward. Do not use port 8765; that is David's own viewer. Report with pictures from your own port and say which port it is.
- You own the eye files: the crate `head_eyes`, and `eyes.rs` and `eye_curves.rs` in `head_sliders`. Do not change the shared kit (`lib.rs`, `feature.rs`, `maker.rs`, `measure.rs`, `set.rs`, `heads.rs`, `mesh.rs`, `bin/sliders.rs`, `head_sliders/Cargo.toml`) or `hq/viewer2/` without telling the HQ first, because those lines are the ones that make a merge hard. If you need one, ask the HQ and it will commit the change first.
- Write everything that ships in the mod in Rust, and everything heavy to compute in Rust too. Python is only for reading files, preparing inputs and gluing steps together. Run it as `.venv/bin/python -I` from `~/.claude/tools/likeness-fit/`.
- For cargo, put `$HOME/.rustup/toolchains/1.98.1-aarch64-apple-darwin/bin` first on the PATH.
- Test on the base head and the 57 KOTOR test heads only. Never on random slider settings.
- Look at pictures of a result before you believe its numbers. Every fault found so far was in the targets or the method, not in the sliders.
- Do not run `git clean -xdf` in the head-paint worktree. It holds 829 MB of viewer reference data that git ignores.
- You do this task without asking for a go-ahead. Stop for the HQ only when something goes against this brief, or when a choice would change what is being built. Ask David, through the HQ, before anything that changes the shared base mesh's topology.
